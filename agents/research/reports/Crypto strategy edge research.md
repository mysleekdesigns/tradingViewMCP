# Stop timing one coin; start ranking many

The evidence says the lab's "no edge found" across about 3,100 trials is the expected result, not a failure of method. Long/flat, price-only timing of BTC or ETH on 5m–4h bars is the hardest setting in the crypto literature. Snooping-corrected studies find no out-of-sample predictability for Bitcoin itself, and at 0.3% round trip the hurdle is about 2σ of a 5-minute move but only 0.11σ of a daily one. The few effects with credible, cost-aware support differ in kind from what has been tested. The best supported is **cross-sectional momentum among liquid coins** (ranking coins against each other, weekly). Next come **portfolio-level time-series momentum on daily bars with multi-week lookbacks**, and derivatives, options and on-chain data used as **crash-risk or exposure filters** rather than direction signals. Each of these needs owner-made lab changes: a multi-coin point-in-time universe including delisted pairs, new non-kline features, and a richer cost model. Realistic all-in cost for BTC/ETH taker trades at retail size is about 16–22 bps per round trip, so the lab's 30 bps is a fair base and stress case. Turnover control (banding, partial adjustment) is worth more than chasing fee tiers. A realistic target is also lower than the lab's bar. The best published crypto results sit at Sharpe 1.0–1.5 before deflation and before the move to spot long-only, below the lab's Deflated Sharpe bar of about 1.7. A small, pre-registered slate of three or four hypotheses is therefore the right next step, with "no edge found" still the most likely outcome.

## Your null result matches the literature's hardest setting

The lab's failure to find edge in single-symbol technical rules repeats the most rigorous findings on Bitcoin. Hudson and Urquhart applied data-snooping controls to technical rules on two BTC markets and three other coins. They found rules gave "substantially higher risk-adjusted returns than the simple buy-and-hold strategy, showing protection against lengthy and severe drawdowns", yet **"no predictability for Bitcoin in the out-of-sample period"**, while predictability remained in other coins ([Hudson & Urquhart 2021](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/annopr/v297y2021i1d10.1007_s10479-019-03357-1.html)). The strongest counter-example, Deprez and Frömmel's test of **75,360 rules** with multiple-hypothesis selection, finds simple rules can beat buy-and-hold out of sample "especially risk–return wise", that is, mostly through lower risk rather than higher return ([Deprez & Frömmel 2024](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/reveco/v93y2024ipbp858-874.html)). Other moving-average studies earned their excess return **"excluding Bitcoin"** ([Grobys, Ahmed & Sapkota 2020](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1544612319308852)). A pre-registered sweep of **18,630 crypto and FX technical variants found none with a Deflated Sharpe above 0.95** ([SSRN 7594361 via ML-Quant](https://www.ml-quant.com/papers/ssrn/7594361/)), which is the closest published analogue to this lab's ledger.

Costs explain why the intraday rules died even where gross Sharpe looked good. Assuming 50% annual BTC volatility and 365-day annualisation, a 30 bps round trip equals about **1.95σ of a 5-minute bar, 1.1σ of a 15-minute bar, 0.28σ of a 4h bar and 0.11σ of a daily bar**. A 5m rule making three round trips a day pays roughly 330% a year in cost drag; a daily trend rule making 15 round trips a year pays about 4.5%. (This is first-principles arithmetic from the research notes, not a sourced figure, and should be recomputed from the lab's realised per-bar volatility.) The empirical work agrees. Fifteen-minute reversal on 183 Binance pairs is statistically real in 90% of pairs every year since 2021, but **"the gross edge peaks near 1.3 bp per trade against a 5 bp round-trip cost"**, and that 5 bp is far below the lab's 30 ([Kitron & Wengrowicz 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21888)). Intraday Bitcoin time-series momentum is significantly positive gross but unprofitable after costs ([Univ. of Reading manuscript](https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/100181/3/21Sep2021Bitcoin%20Intraday%20Time-Series%20Momentum.R2.pdf)). A live experiment on the Binance BTC perpetual concluded that for taker orders it is hard "to achieve short-term returns in excess of the taker fee" ([Albers et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.18625v2)).

Professionals do not escape this either. The average crypto fund's Sharpe of 1.83 versus 1.19 for BTC came with **"weak statistical evidence of managers' skills"** once cross-correlated fund returns were accounted for ([Bianchi & Babiak](https://ideas.repec.org/p/cer/papers/wp672.html); [Riksbank WP 408](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/rapporter/working-papers/2021/no.-408-on-the-performance-of-cryptocurrency-funds.pdf)). In H1 2023, funds averaged +15% against BTC's +83% ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2023/08/07/bitcoin-remains-king-while-crypto-hedge-funds-get-rekt)). Across the cross-section, anomalies concentrate in micro-caps, short legs and bull markets, "fade over time", and momentum alphas fall **26–53% after transaction costs** ([IRFA 2024](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finana/v94y2024ics1057521924001509.html); [ScienceDirect snippet](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1057521924001509)). The pattern is consistent: where crypto predictability exists, it lives in smaller coins, short positions, early samples or lower frequencies. Spot BTC/ETH, long-only, intraday, post-2020 is the corner of the map where every source finds the least.

## Ranking coins, not timing one, carries the strongest evidence

The best-replicated effect in crypto is **cross-sectional momentum among investable coins**, and the lab has never tested it because the engine trades one symbol at a time. Fieberg, Günther, Poddig and Zaremba ran **20,736 research designs** and found analyst-choice dispersion larger than standard errors, yet "size and momentum remain consistently robust across numerous specifications" ([Fieberg et al. 2024](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finana/v92y2024ics1057521924000383.html)). The survivorship-free Open Crypto Pricing project (667 weeks, 249,024 factor variants) shows that once breakpoints are restricted to coins worth at least $100M, **"the size premium vanishes, and momentum remains, next to the market, the factor that prices investable coins"**. It publishes the weekly market, size and momentum series as free CSVs ([Open Crypto Pricing](https://opencryptopricing.com/)). A newer multi-horizon "trend factor" built from price and volume indicators, CTREND, "survives the impact of transaction costs and persists in big and liquid coins" ([Fieberg et al., JFQA 2025](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/trend-factor-for-the-cross-section-of-cryptocurrency-returns/4C1509ACBA33D5DCAF0AC24379148178)). Daily reversal, by contrast, is a liquidity-provision premium: the largest, most tradeable coins show **"daily momentum rather than a reversal"** ([Zaremba et al. 2021](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finana/v78y2021ics1057521921002349.html)). That fits the lab's failed short-horizon reversal runs.

The evidence is contested, and the owner should weigh that before spending budget. Grobys and co-authors, using the top 30 coins weekly over 2016–2023, find momentum earned 1.74% a week before July 2020 but was only significant at 10%, and was **negative and insignificant after July 2020**. They add that "even a single cryptocurrency can cause insignificant momentum portfolio returns"; volatility scaling reduced the crashes ([Grobys et al. 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11408-025-00474-9)). Correcting for delisting returns produces a **62% a year survivorship bias for equal-weighted buy-and-hold** and, in that paper, momentum and beta no longer price returns ([Ammann, Burdorf, Liebi & Stöckl](https://www.sebastianstoeckl.com/publications/wp2022_liebi_survivorship/)). Those authors' results conflict with Fieberg et al. and Open Crypto Pricing, apparently because of universe, breakpoint and delisting choices. That is exactly the fork the lab must fix in advance. Published long-short Sharpe ratios also do not transfer directly: Binance spot cannot short, so the lab can only hold the top quantile against an equal- or value-weighted universe benchmark, and much published momentum alpha comes from the short leg ([IRFA 2024](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finana/v94y2024ics1057521924001509.html)).

Time-series momentum works in the published record at a very different speed from the lab's tests. Han, Kang and Ryu, using 2014 to August 2023 data with a 15 bps cost and modelled liquidation risk, find that buying the market when its look-back return is in the top third of its history beats the market across many settings. **A 28-day lookback with a 5-day hold gives Sharpe 1.51 against 0.84 for the market** ([Han, Kang & Ryu](https://acfr.aut.ac.nz/__data/assets/pdf_file/0009/918729/Time_Series_and_Cross_Sectional_Momentum_in_the_Cryptocurrency_Market_with_IA.pdf); [SSRN 4675565](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4675565)). The same work warns that many cross-sectional momentum portfolios become insignificant once real frictions are included. Much of trend's advantage over buy-and-hold comes from sidestepping a few large drawdowns. One practitioner test found a vol-managed equal-risk BTC/ETH/SOL portfolio with no trend signal reached Sharpe 1.24, against 0.79 for multi-horizon trend plus vol scaling, though with costs of only 1–4 bps ([Summitward](https://summitward.com/learn/crypto-trend-following)). A single-asset daily BTC trend rule is therefore likely to land in the same return family as the lab's earlier MA tests, which the ledger would group as one hypothesis (`family_return_corr`). The novelty must come from the portfolio, not from a new lookback.

Diversification across coins helps less than intuition suggests. Man Group reports average high-frequency pairwise correlation of about 0.6 and finds trend Sharpe peaks at **about 10–15 coins**, fewer for faster models, before liquidity falls off beyond the top 15 ([Man Group](https://www.man.com/insights/in-crypto-we-trend)). At ρ = 0.6, an equal-risk portfolio of same-Sharpe sleeves gains at most about 1.29× in Sharpe however many coins are added (about 1.12× for two, 1.20× for five). Vendor research on a rotational top-20 Donchian ensemble reports Sharpe above 1.5 and 10.8% annual alpha over BTC net of fees, but the universe construction needs point-in-time care and the cost assumptions were not retrieved ([Concretum](https://concretumgroup.com/catching-crypto-trends-a-tactical-approach-for-bitcoin-and-altcoins/)).

Cross-coin lead-lag is the one remaining relative signal with peer-reviewed Binance evidence. Guo, Sang, Tu and Wang find lagged returns of BTC and other coins predict altcoins, with a long-short portfolio earning **"a sizable return out-of-sample after accounting for transaction costs"** ([Guo et al., JEDC 2024](https://smusg.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/cross-cryptocurrency-return-predictability/)). But a Journal of Empirical Finance paper finds the **opposite sign**, with large coins *negatively* predicting others ([JEF 2023](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0927539823000956)), and a 2026 working paper argues BTC's predictability "is not profitability" ([Khan, SSRN 7485359](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7485359)). With the sign unknown and the effect strongest at short horizons in small caps, it ranks behind momentum.

## Derivatives, options and on-chain data inform risk, not direction

The documented *returns* in the derivatives family mostly need a short perpetual leg, which a long/flat spot lab cannot hold. Crypto carry averaged above 10% a year and reached up to 60%, driven by trend-chasing retail demand for leverage, and **"a high crypto carry predicts future price crashes"** ([BIS WP 1087](https://www.bis.org/publications/working-paper-1087-crypto-carry)). But the carry itself has been arbitraged down: perp-spot deviations shrank almost 80% after 2022 ([He, Manela, Ross & von Wachter](https://arxiv.org/html/2212.06888v7)). Binance's own funding archive shows **BTCUSDT funding summed to 30.6% in 2021 but 4.2% in 2022, 5.1% in 2025 and about 2.9% annualised in 2026 to September**. Crucially, **0.0% of BTC and ETH funding prints exceeded the 0.01%-per-8h baseline in 2022, 2025 and 2026** ([data.binance.vision fundingRate](https://data.binance.vision/?prefix=data/futures/um/monthly/fundingRate/BTCUSDT/)). A "go flat when funding is overheated" rule would therefore have stayed fully invested for most of the recent sample, so any backtest result would rest on a handful of 2020–2021 and 2024 episodes. Single-asset funding changes show "near-zero correlation… zero R-squared" with next-period BTC moves ([Presto Research](https://www.prestolabs.io/research/can-funding-rate-predict-price-change)). The better-behaved feature is the continuous **premium index** (perp vs index), archived as klines at every interval from January 2020 ([data.binance.vision premiumIndexKlines](https://data.binance.vision/?prefix=data/futures/um/monthly/premiumIndexKlines/BTCUSDT/1h/)). It maps directly onto the BIS crash-risk channel.

Data that looked unavailable turns out to be free. The REST open-interest endpoint keeps only the latest month ([Binance docs](https://developers.binance.com/docs/derivatives/usds-margined-futures/market-data/rest-api/Open-Interest-Statistics)). The **`metrics` archive**, though, holds 5-minute OI, top-trader and global long/short ratios and perp taker buy/sell ratios for **BTCUSDT from 2020-09-01 with no missing days, and for ETHUSDT from 2021-12-01** ([data.binance.vision metrics](https://data.binance.vision/?prefix=data/futures/um/daily/metrics/BTCUSDT/)). The files need deduplicating and sorting on load, and need a lag of at least one step because their publication latency is unverified. Two gotchas: `fapi.binance.com` returns HTTP 451 from the owner's machine, so the archive is the only practical path, and 2026 spot archive timestamps are in microseconds rather than milliseconds ([data.binance.vision spot klines](https://data.binance.vision/?prefix=data/spot/daily/klines/BTCUSDT/1h/)). Liquidation signals cannot be backtested honestly. USD-M futures have no liquidation archive, and live streams push only the largest liquidation per second ([Binance liquidation streams](https://developers.binance.com/docs/derivatives/usds-margined-futures/websocket-market-streams/Liquidation-Order-Streams)).

Order-flow imbalance has the most consistent directional support, but it is small. On Binance perps, imbalance at quarter-hour openings predicts 4–12h returns, yet the realised forecast "averages about 0.5 bp per boundary, about one tenth of a single standard-tier taker fee" ([Kim & Hansen 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.09426v2)). Daily imbalance on Bitstamp predicts next-day BTC returns with t = 6.2 but **R² of only about 1%** ([RQFA 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10040314/)). Taker-buy volume ships in every Binance spot kline, so it is the cheapest new feature to try, but only as a conditioner on a slow entry. The lab's no-look-ahead rule matters here because imbalance and same-bar return are mechanically correlated. Options data follows the same pattern: implied measures improve volatility forecasts, but **"return direction is not robustly predictable"** ([FRL 2026](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1544612326013814)). Deribit's DVOL is free via a keyless public API from 2021-03-24 ([Deribit API](https://docs.deribit.com/api-reference/upcoming/market-data/public-get_volatility_index_data)), enough for a volatility-regime filter.

On-chain evidence is best for slow cycle timing and has almost no statistical power. The strongest peer-reviewed result raises Sharpe from **0.45 for buy-and-hold to 1.28 with an MVRV Z-score rule**, beating random-entry simulations, over 2013–2025 ([Grobys, Näsman & Sandretto 2026](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/riibaf/v89y2026ics0275531926002138.html)). The sample has only **three complete cycles**, the thresholds used come from practitioners' charts of those same cycles ([Securities.io summary](https://www.securities.io/using-on-chain-data-predict-bitcoin-cycles/)), and 1.28 is still below the lab's 1.7 bar. Inverse NVT and similar ratios "have little predictive power for short-term bitcoin returns" ([Liu & Zhang](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.12893)). MVRV is free and keyless from the CoinMetrics Community API under CC BY-NC (fine for a personal lab). The same probe found SOPR, NVT and realized cap blocked, and exchange flows returned with a provisional "flash" status ([CoinMetrics API](https://community-api.coinmetrics.io/v4/timeseries/asset-metrics)). Entity-labelled flow metrics get recomputed when labels change, which Glassnode itself says "can cause a strong look-ahead bias" ([Glassnode](https://research.glassnode.com/introducing-point-in-time-data/)). Exclude them.

Calendar effects have mostly decayed or never cleared costs. The 21:00–23:00 UTC BTC window earned 33–41% a year gross in-sample with costs "not reported" ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/the-seasonality-of-bitcoin/); [strategy page](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/intraday-seasonality-in-bitcoin)). At 365 round trips that is about 110% a year in cost drag. The Bitcoin Monday effect "does not persist in data after 2015" ([Mueller 2024](https://econpapers.repec.org/article/eeefinlet/v_3a64_3ay_3a2024_3ai_3ac_3as1544612324004598.htm)). FOMC and CPI raise volatility without a consistent direction ([FRL 2026](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1544612326006021)), and the CPI reaction is confined to the first hour ([Block Scholes](https://www.blockscholes.com/institutional-research/is-bitcoin-showing-greater-sensitivity-to-us-cpi-releases-again)). CME gaps disappeared when CME moved to 24/7 trading on 29 May 2026 ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/05/28/bitcoin-s-famous-cme-gaps-are-about-to-disappear-though-three-remain-unresolved)). Halving abnormal returns flipped sign in cycle four ([Lim, SSRN 6589402](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6589402)) and rest on four events. What persists and has strengthened is *activity* seasonality, such as the NY fixing window and weekend volume falling to a 16% share ([Kaiko](https://www.kaiko.com/resources/btc-etfs-impact-on-spot-market-structure)). That is useful for volatility sizing, not entries.

## Real costs are 16–22 bps, and turnover matters more than fees

The lab's 30 bps round trip is a fair conservative base, not an inflated one. Binance VIP0 spot charges **0.10% maker and taker, or 0.075% each when paid in BNB** ([Toolworks](https://tradetoolworks.com/en/cost/binance-fees/); [BitDegree](https://www.bitdegree.org/crypto/tutorials/binance-fees)). BTC/FDUSD and ETH/FDUSD keep **0% maker fees** after standard taker fees returned for all users on 29 January 2026 ([Binance announcement](https://www.binance.com/en/support/announcement/detail/4856a6d4e4014d4e8a5a29ec5fb44857)). Binance leads BTC depth at every level, with over $1M per side within ±0.01% ([CoinGecko 2025](https://www.coingecko.com/research/publications/crypto-liquidity-report-2025)). At $1k–$100k the spread and impact for BTC/ETH are therefore a fraction of a basis point, and a taker round trip costs about **15–20 bps in fees plus a small buffer**. The lab's extra 5 bps a side covers the gap between bar t+1's open print and an achievable fill, and spread widening in volatile bars. For comparison, Binance.US cut spot fees to 0% maker and 0.02% taker in April 2026 ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260422787826/en/Binance.US-Slashes-Spot-Trading-Fees-to-Near-Zero-for-All-Users)), but it historically had the widest USD spreads because it struggled to attract market makers ([Kaiko](https://www.kaiko.com/resources/a-cheatsheet-for-bid-ask-spreads)). Its spread term would need measuring, not assuming.

Maker routing looks cheaper than it is. Limit-order fills are adversely selected: on the Binance BTC perp, fill probability is negatively correlated with subsequent return, so fills come when the signal is wrong and misses when it is right ([Albers et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.18625v2)). A simulation of real short-horizon signals found passive fill rates of **8–25%**, with taker execution earning +4.9 bps on signals a limit would have missed and −4.3 bps on ones it would have filled ([The Microstructure Lab](https://themicrostructurelab.substack.com/p/execution-simulation-for-hf-directional), pseudonymous). Those results are sub-minute; at 4h/daily the larger cost is missed breakouts. An honest bar-level limit model fills only when price trades *through* the level, not merely touches it, and reports P&L at both the conservative and optimistic ends. A strategy should be promoted only on the conservative end ([marketmaker.cc](https://www.marketmaker.cc/en/blog/post/fill-simulation-partial-fills-backtest/)). At VIP0 on USDT pairs, where maker and taker fees are equal, the saving is only a sub-basis-point half-spread.

Turnover control is the better lever. Novy-Marx and Velikov find a **buy/hold spread (no-trade band) is the most effective cost mitigation** of the techniques they test. Most equity anomalies with one-sided monthly turnover below 50% stay significant net of costs, while cutting trading frequency by two-thirds cuts costs by only about one-third ([Novy-Marx & Velikov](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2535173); [NBER PDF](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w20721/w20721.pdf)). Gârleanu and Pedersen show the optimal policy trades only partway toward an aim portfolio when costs bind ([NBER w15205](https://www.nber.org/papers/w15205)). Vol targeting needs the same treatment, because daily rescaling of a 50%-vol asset generates many small trades. Banding cannot create edge; it only keeps more of an edge that already exists gross. Bands and partial-adjustment rates should be fixed from the cost model, because sweeping them adds trials.

The cost-model changes are owner work. A three-level cost grid would help: 20 bps as realistic BNB-paid taker, 30 bps as base, and 40–60 bps as stress. Add an explicit trade-through limit-fill mode, and report break-even cost (the round-trip cost at which net Sharpe reaches zero) for every candidate. A rule requiring break-even cost of at least twice the base cost would *tighten* the criteria, in the spirit of never relaxing them. Because `lab/lab/engine/` and `lab/config/success_criteria*` are under the lab lock, the owner must make these changes; Claude can only propose them.

## Four pre-registered hypotheses and an honest target

The evidence supports a short slate of structurally new hypotheses, each fixed in full before its first backtest. Every row below needs at least one owner-made lab change. The lab currently trades one symbol from klines, with a locked engine, feature registry and success criteria.

The deprioritised list is longer and as important. Anything intraday is out, as are size and short-term reversal (premia live in illiquid coins), hour-of-day and weekday rules, FOMC/CPI direction, halving timing, CME gaps, Google Trends (revised, non-point-in-time samples), liquidation signals (no honest history), exchange netflows (label revisions) and funding carry (needs a short leg). If the owner wants one calendar test, the cheapest honest one is turn-of-month on the textbook (−1, +3) window. It costs about 12 trades a year, but detecting a 1% edge would need roughly 150–200 events, or 12–16 years. BTC's Binance history is about 9 years, so expect no answer either way. The only published BTC/ETH turn-of-month evidence picked its window by optimisation over 2018–2021 ([Vasileiou, SSRN 4230561](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4230561)). The Open Crypto Pricing CMOM series is a free external cross-check for any H1 result.

Realistic targets follow from the decay record. In equities, anomalies are 26% weaker out of sample and 58% weaker after publication ([QuantPedia on McLean–Pontiff](https://quantpedia.com/in-sample-vs-out-of-sample-analysis-of-trading-strategies/)). Converting long-short to spot long-only loses more, and the top published crypto timing results sit at Sharpe 1.0–1.5 before deflation. A spot, long-only, net-of-30-bps implementation is therefore plausibly **below Sharpe 1 and hard to tell apart from vol-targeted buy-and-hold**. The lab's DSR bar of about 1.7 is above what the best credible papers report, which makes "no edge found" the expected outcome for H1–H4 under the current criteria. That bar should not move. Each new plan raises it further, although the expected maximum of N null trials grows only with roughly √(2 ln N), so a small slate costs little. What the evidence does support is a separate question the owner could pre-register in its own criteria file, before any run: does a single, low-frequency overlay cut maximum drawdown versus buy-and-hold without a significant Sharpe loss, with break-even cost at least twice the base? Hudson and Urquhart, Deprez and Frömmel, and Grobys et al. all describe their surviving results in exactly those risk terms. A pass on that question should be reported as a risk overlay, never as an edge. Any candidate from either question still needs 4–8 weeks of `--dry-run` paper trading before it counts as successful.

## Conclusion

The research changes the problem's shape more than its answer. The lab spent its 3,100 trials on the one axis, faster single-symbol timing, where theory and evidence both said costs would win. The unexplored axes are breadth (ranking many coins), slowness (weekly decisions with banded turnover) and information outside klines (premium index, OI, DVOL, MVRV). Those are mostly engine and data problems the owner must solve, not ideas Claude can sweep. That makes the next step an infrastructure decision. A survivorship-free, point-in-time multi-coin universe from the Binance archive is the single change that unlocks the best-evidenced hypotheses, and it also guards against the 62%-a-year equal-weight bias that would otherwise produce a false positive.

The second implication is about what success can mean. Almost every credible positive result in this literature is a risk result: smaller drawdowns, crash avoidance, volatility shaping. Almost none is a return result. A lab that can only declare "edge" against a 1.7 DSR bar will keep returning honest nulls. If the owner wants the lab to answer the questions the evidence can answer, the honest move is a separately pre-registered risk-overlay question with its own fixed criteria, kept clearly apart from the edge question and never used to relax it.
