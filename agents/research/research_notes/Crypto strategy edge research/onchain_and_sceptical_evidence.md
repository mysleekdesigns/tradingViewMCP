# On-chain / fundamental signals for BTC/ETH, and the sceptical evidence on systematic crypto strategies

Researched 2026-10-26. Web content treated as untrusted data; no prompt-injection attempts were observed in any fetched page. ScienceDirect and Wiley full texts were blocked by robots.txt, so several findings rest on abstracts (RePEc/SSRN/arXiv) and search-result snippets of the publisher page; that is noted where it matters. The free-data findings in Q2 were checked by calling the APIs directly on 2026-10-26.

## Q1. Which on-chain metrics have credible out-of-sample predictive evidence, at what horizon, and with how many independent signals?

### Takeaway
No on-chain metric has strong, replicated out-of-sample evidence that survives a multiple-testing correction. The best peer-reviewed support is for cost-basis valuation metrics (MVRV Z-score, NUPL, CVDD) as **cycle-timing** signals over months. But that rests on about 3 complete BTC cycles, so the number of independent signals is tiny. The best published result (Sharpe 1.28 vs 0.45 for buy-and-hold) is still below the lab's Deflated Sharpe bar of about 1.7. SOPR, exchange netflows, hash ribbons and active addresses have mostly vendor and media chart evidence. Stablecoin issuance has peer-reviewed evidence only for the 2017 boom and very short horizons.

### Cited Findings
**MVRV Z-score / NUPL / CVDD (cost-basis valuation). The strongest peer-reviewed evidence, 2026:**
- Grobys, Näsman & Sandretto (2026), *Research in International Business and Finance* 89, "Using on-chain data to predict Bitcoin cycles". They test NUPL, MVRV Z-score and CVDD as simple rule-based strategies. In their words, the indicators "outperform both a buy-and-hold benchmark and random-entry strategies simulated through Monte Carlo analysis… the Sharpe ratio increases from 0.45 for the buy-and-hold benchmark to 1.28 when using the MVRV Z-score" — [RePEc abstract](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/riibaf/v89y2026ics0275531926002138.html)
- Sample: 7 Dec 2013 to 12 Apr 2025, "three complete market cycles: 2015, 2018, and 2022". The MVRV Z-score rules use an entry below about −0.2 and an exit threshold of 5–7 — [Securities.io summary of the paper](https://www.securities.io/using-on-chain-data-predict-bitcoin-cycles/) (secondary source; the thresholds should be checked against the paper)
- Same paper: the CVDD strategies identified cycle bottoms but held positions too long. The summary reports a random-entry comparison "p-value of 99%" for CVDD annualised performance, meaning it did **not** beat random entries on that measure — [Securities.io](https://www.securities.io/using-on-chain-data-predict-bitcoin-cycles/)
- The publisher abstract describes the approach as "deliberately parsimonious and interpretable", with MVRV Z-score best "across all versions tested" — [ScienceDirect snippet](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0275531926002138)
- Older work (2022) on oscillators built from crypto-asset pricing methods cites the convention that MVRV above 3.7 means overvalued and below 1 means undervalued. These thresholds come from the vendor and practitioner literature, not from out-of-sample tests — [MDPI Information 13(12):560](https://www.mdpi.com/2078-2489/13/12/560)

**NVT and "fundamental-to-market" ratios:**
- Liu & Zhang, "Cryptocurrency Valuation: An Explainable AI Approach": existing fundamental-to-market ratios, including the inverse of NVT, "have little predictive power for short-term bitcoin returns". Their new price-to-utility (PU) ratio "effectively predicts long-term bitcoin returns". Horizons tested were 1 week, 30, 90, 180 and 360 days. This is a single study by the authors who proposed the PU ratio; last revised 2023 — [arXiv 2201.12893](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.12893), [HTML version](https://arxiv.org/html/2201.12893v5)

**Network adoption and production factors (time-series predictors):**
- Liu & Tsyvinski (2021, RFS): crypto returns are predicted by crypto-specific factors. They find "a strong time-series momentum effect", and "proxies for investor attention strongly forecast future cryptocurrency returns". They also build network (user adoption) and production-cost factors. Older-only evidence (sample ends about 2018) — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v34y2021i6p2689-2727..html)

**Machine learning on on-chain features (2026):**
- *Investment Analysts Journal* (2026): rolling-window ML forecasts of **daily** BTC returns, 2017–2023. On-chain variables "account for approximately 50 per cent of total forecasting contribution, with transaction fees and mining-related metrics ranking among the top", and VIX shows limited relevance. The result is about forecast error and feature importance, not net trading profit — [journal page snippet](https://journals.co.za/doi/10.1080/10293523.2026.2616575)
- A 2025 study (*Engineering Applications of AI*) combines ML/DL with on-chain and technical-analysis inputs to predict BTC direction and magnitude. Only the abstract was seen — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0952197625010875)

**Stablecoin issuance / USDT minting:**
- Griffin & Shams (2020, *Journal of Finance*): during the **2017 boom**, Tether purchases "are timed following market downturns and result in sizable increases in Bitcoin prices". The flow is "attributable to one entity", and the authors read it as supply-driven (unbacked money) inflation, not investor demand. The finding is tied to one era — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v75y2020i4p1913-1964.html)
- Ante, Fiedler & Strehle (2021, *Finance Research Letters*): event study of 565 issuances across 7 stablecoins, Apr 2019 – Mar 2020. Markets fall in the week before issuance, with positive abnormal returns in the **24 hours** around it. Effects "remain insignificant for some stablecoin subsamples", and issuance size does not matter. Older-only, one-year sample, 24-hour horizon — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finlet/v41y2021ics1544612320316810.html)
- The direction of causality is contested: Griffin & Shams argue causal manipulation, while Wei (2018) and others argue against it — [arXiv 2507.13883 survey](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.13883)

**Hash ribbons / miner capitulation:**
- I found only media and vendor evidence. For example, CoinDesk says the signal "has historically aligned with strong bitcoin-price bottom conditions" — [CoinDesk 2025-11-27](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2025/11/27/hash-ribbon-flashes-signal-that-often-marks-cyclical-bottoms-for-bitcoin-price), [CoinDesk 2026-02-25](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/02/25/one-of-longest-mining-capitulations-nears-end-signaling-potential-btc-price-bottom). Definition: the 30-day hash-rate moving average crosses below the 60-day — [quantum-algo guide](https://www.quantum-algo.com/blog/guides/hash-ribbons/)

**Exchange netflows / ETF flows:**
- I found no peer-reviewed out-of-sample study of exchange netflows. Vendor definitions only — [CryptoQuant](https://cryptoquant.com/asset/btc/chart/exchange-flows/exchange-netflow-total)
- Glassnode says address clustering and manual label additions "entail full historic recomputation of a metric… [which] can cause a strong look-ahead bias". It introduced immutable point-in-time (PiT) metrics for backtesting — [Glassnode research, 2023](https://research.glassnode.com/introducing-point-in-time-data/), [Glassnode PiT docs](https://docs.glassnode.com/data/point-in-time-metrics)
- ETF flows: FalconX finds a flow shock has a peak effect of about 1.2% on days 3–4, then decays. This is a "preliminary statistical investigation", not peer-reviewed — [FalconX](https://www.falconx.io/newsroom/what-can-spot-etf-flows-tell-us-about-the-trajectory-of-bitcoin-prices-a-preliminary-statistical-investigation). An SSRN paper reports ETF net flows predicting bitcoin price **levels** with R² of 95%. A levels-on-levels R² is a classic spurious-regression red flag — [Mazur & Polyzos, SSRN 5452994](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/Delivery.cfm/5452994.pdf?abstractid=5452994&mirid=1&type=2)

**SOPR / active addresses:**
- The only academic hit treats SOPR and NUPL as correlates of volatility regimes, not tested return predictors — [PeerJ CS-1750](https://peerj.com/articles/cs-1750/). Other hits were vendor or education pages (e.g. [blockchain-council](https://www.blockchain-council.org/bitcoin/bitcoin-on-chain-analysis/)) or AI-generated news ([ainvest](https://www.ainvest.com/news/bitcoin-chain-signals-decoding-institutional-buying-market-sentiment-2025-2509/), unreliable).

### Inferences
- **Statistical power is the binding problem.** Cycle-timing signals (MVRV Z, NUPL, CVDD, hash ribbons) fire roughly once per 4-year cycle. A 2013–2025 sample has about 3 tops and 3 bottoms, so a Sharpe gain from those few trades cannot be told apart from luck by any standard test. The random-entry Monte Carlo in Grobys et al. helps, but it does not correct for the many thresholds and metrics the field has tried on the same 3 cycles. The −0.2 and 5–7 thresholds were themselves known from practitioners' charts of those same cycles, which is look-ahead in the choice of thresholds.
- The best published on-chain result (Sharpe 1.28 vs 0.45) falls short of the lab's Deflated Sharpe bar of about 1.7 even before costs and multiple-testing deflation. The 0.45 buy-and-hold Sharpe also looks low for BTC over 2013–2025. Bianchi & Babiak report a BTC buy-and-hold Sharpe of 1.19 on an earlier window (see Q3), so the benchmark depends heavily on start date. That makes the comparison fragile.
- MVRV Z-score is the most defensible candidate to test, as a slow regime or exposure filter (e.g. scale BTC exposure down when MVRV-Z is extreme). It is not a trading signal; expect at most a drawdown reduction. Treat it as **one** hypothesis with one holdout check, not a family to sweep.
- Exchange-flow metrics built on entity labels are unsafe to backtest from non-PiT sources: the labels are revised after the fact, so a backtest on them has look-ahead. This matches the lab's point-in-time constraint.
- Short-horizon effects (stablecoin issuance within 24h, ETF flows within days) are small, era-specific, and would need intraday flow data that the lab does not have.

### Gaps
- I could not read the Grobys et al. (2026) full text to check whether they hold out a period, how many parameter variants they tried, or whether results are net of costs.
- I found no peer-reviewed out-of-sample study for SOPR, exchange netflows, hash ribbons or active addresses alone. Not finding one is itself signal.
- "A Comprehensive Look at Bitcoin Return Predictability" (Sanhueza et al., 2025) looks relevant, but the full text was behind ResearchGate — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/396908181_A_Comprehensive_Look_at_Bitcoin_Return_Predictability)
- I found no on-chain studies specific to ETH (MVRV etc.).

## Q2. Free historical sources and their licensing

### Takeaway
CoinMetrics Community API (keyless, CC BY-NC 4.0, daily) is the best free source. It **does** serve BTC/ETH MVRV, market cap, exchange in/outflows and exchange supply, hash rate and active addresses. It does **not** serve realized cap, NVT, SOPR, adjusted transfer value or miner revenue. Blockchain.com charts and DefiLlama stablecoins are free and keyless. The Glassnode free tier is daily, Tier-1 only, and has no API; point-in-time data is Professional-only.

### Cited Findings
- CoinMetrics Community HTTP API: root `https://community-api.coinmetrics.io/v4`, "no API key required… available to the community under the Creative Commons license". The Python client itself is Apache 2.0 — [CoinMetrics API reference](https://gitbook-docs.coinmetrics.io/api-reference)
- Community data is "available for free for non-commercial use under a Creative Commons license" — [CoinMetrics API conventions](https://gitbook-docs.coinmetrics.io/access-our-data/api). Licence: CC BY-NC 4.0 — [apis.io](https://apis.io/providers/coin-metrics/), [coinsrate (checked 2026-10-09)](https://www.coinsrate.org/partner-content/coin-metrics-data/)
- Rate limit: "10 requests per 6 seconds sliding window" per IP, no client support. Community Atlas is limited to 30 days of history, and community reference rates to the last 7 points at fine resolution — [CoinMetrics Community Data](https://gitbook-docs.coinmetrics.io/packages/coin-metrics-community-data). That page gives the root as `api.coinmetrics.io/v4`, which conflicts with the API reference's `community-api.coinmetrics.io`. The latter worked in my test.
- **Direct API probe, 2026-10-26, BTC, 1d frequency:** free: `CapMVRVCur`, `CapMrktCurUSD`, `FlowInExNtv`, `FlowOutExNtv`, `SplyExNtv`, `HashRate`, `AdrActCnt`. Forbidden ("not available with supplied credentials"): `CapRealUSD`, `NVTAdj`, `TxTfrValAdjUSD`, `SOPR`, `RevUSD` — [community API endpoint](https://community-api.coinmetrics.io/v4/timeseries/asset-metrics). The catalog lists `CapMVRVCur` at 1d for many assets including ETH — [catalog](https://community-api.coinmetrics.io/v4/catalog-all/asset-metrics?metrics=CapMVRVCur)
- The exchange-flow metrics came back with `"FlowInExNtv-status":"flash"` and a status time. That marks them as provisional values, not final — [community API endpoint](https://community-api.coinmetrics.io/v4/timeseries/asset-metrics)
- Blockchain.com charts API: keyless JSON, e.g. `api.blockchain.info/charts/hash-rate?timespan=all&format=json`. It returns daily hash rate from 2009-01-03 (probed 2026-10-26) — [Blockchain.com charts API](https://www.blockchain.com/explorer/api/charts_api)
- DefiLlama stablecoins API: `GET /stablecoincharts/all`, `/stablecoin/{asset}`, `/stablecoinchains`, and others — [apis.io DefiLlama](https://apis.io/apis/defillama/defillama-stablecoins-api/), [DefiLlama API docs](https://api-docs.defillama.com/). The probe of `stablecoins.llama.fi/stablecoincharts/all?stablecoin=1` (USDT) returned daily data from 2017-11-29.
- Glassnode free tier: "Tier 1 metrics… at 24-hour resolution", delayed. The $49 Advanced plan has 4 years of history — [Spark comparison](https://www.spark.money/tools/bitcoin-onchain-analytics-comparison), [Spark pricing](https://www.spark.money/tools/crypto-api-pricing-comparison) (third-party). Glassnode's own docs say the API is for Professional subscribers, with only a "Light API" for Advanced — [Glassnode API docs](https://docs.glassnode.com/basic-api/api). The pricing page says API access is a Professional add-on and that access levels differ in "resolution, history depth, and access to Point-in-Time data" — [Glassnode pricing](https://studio.glassnode.com/pricing)

### Inferences
- An MVRV-based filter is buildable for free from CoinMetrics (`CapMVRVCur`, `CapMrktCurUSD`). The Z-score needs the standard deviation of market cap, which can be computed from `CapMrktCurUSD`. Realized cap can be backed out as market cap divided by MVRV, so the missing `CapRealUSD` is not a blocker. The Z-score must use an expanding or rolling window, never the full-sample standard deviation (vendor charts usually use the full sample, which is look-ahead).
- CC BY-NC suits a personal, non-commercial lab. Store the data locally with attribution and do not redistribute.
- CoinMetrics exchange flows are labelled-entity metrics with "flash" status. They are not point-in-time, so they carry the Glassnode-documented label-revision look-ahead risk. Do not use them in the lab without that caveat.
- SOPR and NVT are not free from CoinMetrics. A free path is to compute them from raw chain data, which is heavy work.

### Gaps
- I did not confirm the earliest available dates for each CoinMetrics community metric: repeated probes failed with connection errors after the first batch, possibly the rate limit or sandbox network.
- I did not find explicit licence terms for Blockchain.com charts data or DefiLlama data.
- I did not verify whether CoinMetrics revises historical MVRV values, i.e. whether it is point-in-time.

## Q3. Sceptical evidence: technical rules with data-snooping corrections, anomaly decay after costs and publication, fund performance vs BTC, retail bot evidence

### Takeaway
After data-snooping corrections, the evidence for price-based rules on **Bitcoin itself** is weak or absent out-of-sample. Hudson & Urquhart found no out-of-sample predictability for BTC. Grobys et al. found profits came from coins other than BTC. The main exception is Deprez & Frömmel (2024): selected rules beat buy-and-hold "especially risk-return wise", meaning mainly through lower risk. Cross-sectional anomalies shrink sharply after costs, concentrate in micro-caps and bull markets, and fade over time. Crypto funds show weak statistical evidence of skill and can lag BTC badly in bull phases.

### Cited Findings
**Technical trading rules with data-snooping control:**
- Hudson & Urquhart (2021, *Annals of Operations Research* 297:191–220), using two BTC markets and three other cryptocurrencies, with data-snooping control (White's Reality Check is cited). Rules offer "substantially higher risk-adjusted returns than the simple buy-and-hold strategy, showing protection against lengthy and severe drawdowns… However there is **no predictability for Bitcoin in the out-of-sample period**, although predictability remains in other cryptocurrency markets." Older data — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/annopr/v297y2021i1d10.1007_s10479-019-03357-1.html), [open-access PDF](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s10479-019-03357-1.pdf)
- Deprez & Frömmel (2024, *International Review of Economics & Finance* 93:858–874): 75,360 simple rules across 6 classes at daily and intraday frequencies. The best rules after costs are selected with a multiple-hypothesis procedure, then combined into portfolios and tested out-of-sample. Simple rules "can outperform a buy-and-hold strategy in the bitcoin market out-of-sample", "especially risk–return wise". This is the strongest recent counter-evidence to the sceptical view — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/reveco/v93y2024ipbp858-874.html). Its reference list includes Sullivan, Timmermann & White (1999), Romano-Shaikh-Wolf (2008) and Bajgrowicz & Scaillet (2012), the FDR-based work showing technical-trading profits vanish after false-discovery control and costs — [same page](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/reveco/v93y2024ipbp858-874.html)
- Grobys, Ahmed & Sapkota (2020, *Finance Research Letters* 32:101396), a variable moving-average rule across cryptocurrencies: "**excluding Bitcoin** the technical trading rule generates an excess return of 8.76% p.a. after controlling for the average market return" — [ScienceDirect snippet](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1544612319308852). Older-only.
- Ahmed, Grobys & Sapkota (2020, FRL), privacy coins: the variable moving-average strategy "is successful only when applied to Dash", at 14.6–18.25% p.a. over buy-and-hold — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1544612320300829). Older-only.
- Corbet, Eraslan, Lucey & Sensoy (2019, FRL 31:32–37): high-frequency BTC returns, "significant support for the moving average strategies"; the variable-length MA performs best. The abstract mentions no data-snooping correction. Older-only — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:finlet:v:31:y:2019:i:c:p:32-37)
- Detzel, Liu, Strauss, Zhou & Zhu (2021, *Financial Management*): bitcoin returns are predictable by 5- to 100-day moving averages of price but "largely unpredictable by macroeconomic variables", with an equilibrium learning model to explain why — [Wiley abstract](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/fima.12310), [citing summary](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/323324259_Bitcoin_Predictability_and_Profitability_via_Technical_Analysis). Older data.
- Gerritsen, Bouri, Ramezanifar & Roubaud (2020, FRL), "The profitability of technical trading rules in the Bitcoin market": exists — [author PDF](http://dirkgerritsen.nl/uploads/gerritsen_et_al_2020_bitcoin_trading_rules.pdf), but I could not extract its results.

**Anomalies after costs, over time, and out-of-sample:**
- "Cryptocurrency anomalies and economic constraints" (2024, *International Review of Financial Analysis* 94), 3,956 coins over 2014–2022. "Size and volume anomalies originate from micro-cap coins of negligible economic importance". Momentum "prevails in larger cryptocurrencies but incurs substantial trading costs and extracts alphas largely from short positions". "Most abnormal returns occur primarily in bull markets and **fade over time**" — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finana/v94y2024ics1057521924001509.html). Momentum alphas "fall by 26% to 53%" after transaction costs — [ScienceDirect snippet](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1057521924001509)
- "Taming crypto anomalies: A Lasso-type factor model" (2026, RIBAF): 49 crypto anomalies following Liu et al. (2022). There are "noticeable changes in the behavior of out-of-sample anomalies" compared with in-sample — [ScienceDirect snippet](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0275531926000255)
- "Cross-sectional interactions in cryptocurrency returns" (2024, IRFA): an out-of-sample long-short strategy has Sharpe above 1, but "low liquidity, which raises transaction costs, can dampen" it — [ScienceDirect snippet](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1057521924007415)
- Equity-market base rate for comparison: anomaly profits "decrease substantially after the publication" (currency anomalies) — [CFA Germany summary](https://www.cfa-germany.de/en/Currency-Anomalies). Chen: the 90th–95th percentile anomalies have bias-adjusted **net** returns of only about 20 bp per month — [Chen, "Accounting for the Anomaly Zoo"](https://jacobslevycenter.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2019/09/Accounting-for-the-Anomaly-Zoo.pdf)

**Backtest overfitting:**
- Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado & Zhu define PBO as "the rate at which optimal IS strategies underperform the median" out-of-sample — [Bailey et al. PDF](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/backtest-prob.pdf)
- A crypto deep-RL paper frames backtest-overfitting detection as a hypothesis test and rejects overfitted agents. Its own reported results should be treated as in-sample claims — [arXiv 2209.05559](https://arxiv.org/html/2209.05559v6)
- Red-flag example: an arXiv preprint claims an out-of-sample Sharpe of 2.41 and max drawdown of −12.7% for crypto trend-following over 2022–2024. It is not peer-reviewed and implausible next to the evidence above — [arXiv 2602.11708](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11708v1)

**Crypto funds vs BTC:**
- Bianchi & Babiak (2022, *Journal of Banking & Finance*). The aggregate average fund's annualised Sharpe was 1.83, against 1.22 for the crypto market portfolio and 1.19 for BTC buy-and-hold — [Riksbank WP 408 (2021)](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/rapporter/working-papers/2021/no.-408-on-the-performance-of-cryptocurrency-funds.pdf). However, "the significance of the alphas becomes statistically weaker after considering the cross-sectional correlation in fund returns" — [SSRN 3559092](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3559092). And "a sizable minority of managers are able to cover their costs and generate large alphas. However, there is **weak statistical evidence of managers' skills**" — [RePEc WP](https://ideas.repec.org/p/cer/papers/wp672.html). Sample is before 2022; fund databases have survivorship and self-reporting bias.
- 21e6 Capital via Bloomberg/CoinDesk, H1 2023: crypto funds averaged +15% vs BTC +83%. Directional funds averaged +22%, market-neutral +6.8%. About 97 funds (13%) of more than 700 tracked closed. "A simple buy-and-hold investment into bitcoin would have outperformed all of these fund baskets" — [CoinDesk 2023-08-07](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2023/08/07/bitcoin-remains-king-while-crypto-hedge-funds-get-rekt)
- A VisionTrack crypto hedge fund performance update (Feb 2024) compares fund indices with BTC buy-and-hold from the Nov 2021 peak — [VisionTrack PDF](https://assets.ctfassets.net/yksdf0mjii3y/6kHSh8SbUt4RX6xHyjCzZN/0249c81c537ecdc30b7a8c28b1799469/February_2024_Crypto_Hedge_Fund_Performance_Update.pdf) (not read in full)

**Retail bots / Freqtrade:**
- I found no peer-reviewed or audited dataset on retail crypto bot or Freqtrade live performance. Claims I found are unverifiable and should **not** be cited as evidence: "only 3% of 500 documented retail bot strategies outperformed buy-and-hold" — [bitconize](https://bitconize.com/articles/automated-crypto-trading-bot-does/); "73% of retail algorithmic accounts lagged S&P 500" — [myfundedcapital](https://myfundedcapital.com/are-trading-bots-profitable/). Both are marketing or blog pages with no traceable primary source.

### Inferences
- **The literature has its own data-snooping problem.** Papers reporting technical-rule profits on crypto are numerous, mostly 2019–2020, mostly in Finance Research Letters, with little out-of-sample testing. The two most rigorous studies (Hudson & Urquhart with a reality check and holdout; Deprez & Frömmel with multiple-hypothesis selection and out-of-sample portfolios) disagree on BTC. Even the positive one frames the edge as risk-return, not raw return.
- The pattern is consistent: where crypto predictability exists, it sits in smaller or less liquid coins, short positions, bull markets, or early sample periods. BTC/ETH spot, long-only, after costs, post-2020 is the hardest setting. That matches the lab's no-edge result over about 3,100 ledgered trials.
- A precise answer to "what fraction of published strategies survive" does not exist for crypto. The closest proxies: the IRFA 2024 finding that anomalies fade over time and shrink 26–53% after costs, Chen's net-return evidence for equities, and Hudson & Urquhart's BTC out-of-sample null.

### Gaps
- I could not access the full texts of Hudson & Urquhart (rule count, exact test details), Gerritsen et al. (2020) or Deprez & Frömmel (2024) (out-of-sample period, cost assumptions, whether results hold after 2022).
- I found no post-2022 study applying White's Reality Check or Hansen's SPA to BTC/ETH technical rules covering the 2022 bear and 2024–25 ETF era.
- I found no credible dataset on Freqtrade, 3Commas or other retail bot outcomes.
- I did not find PwC/AIMA crypto hedge fund report figures for 2024–2025.

## Q4. What does this imply as a realistic target?

### Takeaway
The credible target is **matching buy-and-hold's return per unit of risk with smaller drawdowns**: a long/flat or exposure-scaling overlay judged against buy-and-hold and vol-targeted buy-and-hold. Beating BTC buy-and-hold on raw return with price-only or on-chain rules is not supported by out-of-sample, snooping-corrected evidence. Even the best published on-chain result (Sharpe 1.28) is below the lab's DSR bar of about 1.7.

### Cited Findings
- The positive results that survive scrutiny are framed as risk-adjusted or drawdown benefits: "protection against lengthy and severe drawdowns" with no BTC out-of-sample predictability ([Hudson & Urquhart](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/annopr/v297y2021i1d10.1007_s10479-019-03357-1.html)); outperformance "especially risk–return wise" ([Deprez & Frömmel 2024](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/reveco/v93y2024ipbp858-874.html)); on-chain cycle strategies show "smaller drawdowns" ([Securities.io on Grobys et al. 2026](https://www.securities.io/using-on-chain-data-predict-bitcoin-cycles/))
- Professional managers underperformed BTC by about 68 points in a strong half-year (H1 2023) — [CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2023/08/07/bitcoin-remains-king-while-crypto-hedge-funds-get-rekt)
- Abnormal returns concentrate in bull markets and fade — [IRFA 2024](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finana/v94y2024ics1057521924001509.html)

### Inferences
- **For the lab:** with a DSR bar of about 1.7 over about 3,100 trials, "no edge" is the expected outcome under the literature. The bar is above what the best published crypto timing papers report before deflation. Relaxing the bar is not justified. A better question is whether a *pre-registered, single* low-frequency overlay (e.g. a MVRV-Z exposure filter, or a 5–100 day MA trend filter per Detzel et al.) reduces max drawdown or ulcer index versus buy-and-hold without a significant Sharpe loss. That is a different, pre-declared success criterion, which the project rules say must be fixed before a run.
- **Power warning for on-chain cycle signals:** 2–4 trades per cycle and about 3 cycles in the free data means any DSR or holdout test has almost no power. A holdout window short enough to keep the design window useful may contain zero signals. Such signals are better judged as risk-management overlays on exposure (continuous scaling) than as entry/exit trades.
- Beating buy-and-hold on raw return in a long-only BTC/ETH setting requires being out of the market during big up-moves rarely enough to beat a buy-and-hold Sharpe of about 1 (period-dependent: 0.45 to 1.19 in the sources above). Fund data suggests even professionals rarely do this consistently.

### Gaps
- I found no study that directly reports the fraction of retail or public crypto strategies beating buy-and-hold after costs out-of-sample. The base rate has to be inferred from the proxies above.
- I found no published evaluation of an on-chain signal under a Deflated Sharpe / PBO framework.
